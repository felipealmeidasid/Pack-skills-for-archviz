# Tropical Modern Brasileiro

## Essence

Premium contemporary architecture for warm Brazilian or tropical contexts, built around shade, horizontal volumes, natural materials, garden integration, water, ventilation, and a relaxed but refined lifestyle.

## Architecture

- Low or horizontal massing when possible.
- Deep eaves, terraces, pergolas, brises, muxarabi, cobogo, or shaded glass.
- Living spaces opening toward garden, pool, courtyard, forest, beach, or view.
- Clear transition from street/privacy to open social areas.
- Large openings must make climate sense: protected from direct glare and heat.

## Materials

- Warm wood: ceilings, soffits, decks, brises, joinery.
- Natural stone: floors, walls, landscape edges, textured bases.
- Concrete or pigmented concrete used with restraint.
- Glass with realistic reflections and solar protection.
- Metal in dark, bronze, champagne, or matte black accents.

## Landscape

- Dense but controlled tropical planting.
- Garden as spatial continuation, not decoration.
- Pool or water plane can act as reflection/cooling element.
- Use climate-appropriate species and avoid fake decorative palms.

## Light

- Tropical shade.
- Morning clean light.
- Late afternoon warm light.
- Blue-hour only when architecture has believable warm interior glow.

## Human Behavior

- Barefoot or relaxed premium lifestyle.
- Casual linen, light cotton, subtle resort wardrobe.
- People should open glass doors, sit near shaded terraces, move between living and pool, serve food, read, or walk through garden.

## Best Uses

Luxury homes, coastal houses, forest houses, condominium houses, residential resorts, pool/terrace scenes, and Brazilian premium lifestyle films.
