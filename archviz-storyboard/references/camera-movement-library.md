# Camera Movement Library

Use this reference when the user asks to define video styles, camera movement language, transitions, sound design cues, or a detailed movement plan for archviz and real estate videos.

This library is based on the user's `movimentos_cinematograficos_arquitetura.pdf`, with additional archviz-safe movements added.

## Selection Rules

- Treat this library as movement vocabulary, not as a random checklist. Choose movements because they support the story beat, sales value, architectural reveal, or retention hook.
- Keep variety across the video, but preserve one coherent visual language. A premium house film can mix macro glides, gimbal reveals, sliders, rack focus, waterline moves, and crane pull-backs, but the rhythm should feel directed, not shuffled.
- Use one primary camera movement per shot. Add a secondary movement only when it improves the architectural reveal.
- Prefer smooth, readable movements for strict project fidelity.
- Let the movement reveal architecture, materials, scale, spatial flow, amenities, or views.
- For fast social hooks, allow quick acceleration only when the image remains understandable. Add a brief stable settle on the hero subject before cutting.
- Avoid movements that require unseen geometry, reverse angles, extra rooms, extra facades, or impossible camera paths.
- For Seedance, describe camera movement as the video camera moving through the real scene, not as animation over a storyboard board.
- For a premium style, favor slow push-ins, lateral dollies, crane moves, parallax reveals, glides, and restrained match cuts.
- For a fast social style, use shorter moves, speed ramps, snap zooms, whip pans, cut-on-action, and sharper sound hits.

## Prompt Syntax

Use compact, production-ready language:

```text
CAMERA: slow dolly-in from the pool edge toward the facade, keeping the original balcony rhythm locked.
MOTION: lateral truck right with foreground landscaping parallax, no change to architecture.
TRANSITION: match cut on vertical window mullions into the lobby columns.
SFX: soft cinematic whoosh, subtle low hit on the hero reveal, clean ambient bed.
```

## 1. Basic Camera Moves

- Static Shot: camera does not move. Use for hero architecture, exact facade, luxury calm, or fidelity checks.
- Lock-Off: completely locked camera with no micro movement. Use for exact comparisons, text-free board frames, or final hero frames.
- Push In: camera moves closer to the subject. Use for facade reveal, pool texture, lobby entrance, fireplace, balcony view, or amenity hero.
- Pull Out: camera moves away to reveal context. Use to start on detail and reveal the full room, pool deck, facade, or site.
- Dolly In: physical camera advance. Use for arrival sequences, hallways, decks, porte-cochere, lobby approach, and pool edges.
- Dolly Out: physical camera retreat. Use to leave an interior and reveal facade, balcony, garden, or social area.
- Truck Left: camera slides left. Use across living rooms, facade rhythm, kitchen islands, pool edges, or amenity seating.
- Truck Right: camera slides right. Use for balcony, terrace, pool, lobby desk, wall panels, and long horizontal architecture.
- Pan Left: camera rotates left from a fixed point. Use when the reference supports a wider scene within the same angle.
- Pan Right: camera rotates right from a fixed point. Use for controlled reveal, not for inventing unseen sides.
- Tilt Up: camera points upward. Use from flooring/material detail to ceiling, double-height volume, tower height, or facade top.
- Tilt Down: camera points downward. Use from ceiling, canopy, tower, or horizon down to entrance, pool, plaza, or landscaping.
- Roll: camera rotates around its lens axis. Use rarely for dramatic social edits, not for premium architectural clarity.
- Slider Move: short, precise lateral move. Use for material details, decor, countertops, handrails, and luxury interiors.
- Diagonal Dolly: camera moves forward and sideways. Use for soft parallax through living rooms, terraces, and gardens.

## 2. Zoom And Lens Moves

- Zoom In: lens magnifies the subject. Use for detail emphasis when physical movement would be unsafe.
- Zoom Out: lens widens the view. Use to reveal more context while preserving the same viewpoint.
- Slow Zoom: subtle cinematic zoom. Use for calm premium moments and hero frames.
- Snap Zoom: short fast zoom. Use for Reels, energetic transitions, or feature callouts.
- Crash Zoom: aggressive fast zoom. Use sparingly for waterfalls, fire, amenities, or dramatic social edits.
- Reverse Zoom: zoom direction reverses. Use for stylized material reveals, with caution.
- Dolly Zoom: camera moves while zooming opposite. Use only for a deliberate vertigo effect in corridors or strong axial spaces.
- Rack Focus: focus shifts from foreground to background. Use for foreground vegetation, glass, stone, furniture, or signage details.
- Lens Breathing: subtle lens response during focus. Use as a premium tactile effect, not as a main movement.
- Macro Detail To Wide Reveal: begin on material detail, then pull out or cut to the wider space. Use for marble, wood, water, metal, stone, and lighting.

## 3. Reveals

- Reveal: begins hidden, then reveals the subject. Use behind plants, walls, doors, columns, curtains, pergolas, or landscape.
- Foreground Reveal: passes behind a foreground object. Use with vegetation, columns, water features, furniture, mullions, or screens.
- Parallax Reveal: foreground and background move at different speeds. Use for premium depth in facades, gardens, terraces, and interiors.
- Shadow Reveal: architecture emerges from shadow or shaded foreground. Use for sunset, night, lobby, and facade drama.
- Light Leak: camera crosses a bright light flare or beam. Use sparingly for glossy transitions and premium mood.
- Entry Reveal: camera enters through a door, gate, lobby opening, or threshold. Use only when the next space is referenced.
- Corner Reveal: camera rounds a visible corner. Use for corridors, kitchen to living, lobby to lounge, or facade edge.
- Threshold Reveal: camera crosses from one zone to another. Use for indoor-outdoor integration and balcony connections.
- Curtain Or Blind Reveal: fabric or screen opens the view. Use only when curtains, blinds, brises, or screens exist in the reference.
- Architectural Mask Reveal: a column, mullion, wall edge, or pergola beam wipes the frame. Use for clean archviz transitions.
- Reflection Reveal: reveal through glass, mirror, water, or polished stone. Use only if the reflective surface is clearly present.

## 4. Tracking And Following

- Tracking Shot: camera follows a subject or path. In archviz, use mostly as a path-following camera, not people-first.
- Follow Shot: camera follows from behind. Use for lifestyle only when people are requested.
- Leading Shot: camera moves backward in front of a subject. Use for lifestyle entrances or guided walkthroughs.
- Chase Shot: energetic following shot. Use rarely, mostly for sport, leisure, or high-energy social ads.
- Over Shoulder: camera follows behind a shoulder. Use only if people are requested.
- Profile Tracking: camera follows laterally. Use for walking subjects, poolside lifestyle, or facade rhythm.
- Walkthrough Glide: camera moves like a smooth human walkthrough. Use for interiors, lobbies, corridors, and amenity flows.
- Path Trace: camera follows a visible road, driveway, pool edge, corridor, or garden path. Use for site logic and arrival storytelling.

## 5. Rotational Moves

- Orbit: camera circles around a subject. Use only when the full geometry is known or the orbit is very partial.
- Parallax Orbit: orbit with foreground depth. Use for pools, sculpture, entrance volumes, and landscape frames when references support it.
- Slow Orbit: restrained circular movement. Use for premium hero architecture with known volume.
- Hyper Orbit: fast circular movement. Use sparingly for social edits, and avoid when fidelity matters.
- Arc Shot: camera moves in a half-circle. Use for facade, pool, garden, reception desk, or furniture group.
- Semi-Arc: smaller arc. Safer than a full orbit for single-reference images.
- Pivot: camera rotates around a fixed point. Use to reveal adjacent areas from a visible central point.
- Turnaround: full 360 degrees. Avoid unless the complete environment is supplied.
- Yaw Reveal: controlled horizontal rotation. Use as a more precise alternative to generic pan.

## 6. Drone And Vertical Moves

- Crane Up: camera rises. Use from garden, pool, entrance, plaza, or street to reveal the building or site.
- Crane Down: camera descends. Use from sky, tower top, canopy, or horizon into entrance, pool, or amenity.
- Pedestal Up: camera rises without tilting. Use for vertical facade rhythm, double-height interiors, and towers.
- Pedestal Down: camera descends without tilting. Use for tower-to-ground, lobby volume, and pool deck.
- Top-Down: aerial vertical view. Use for site plans, subdivisions, masterplans, pools, courtyards, and amenity layouts.
- Bird Orbit: elevated orbit. Use only when site geometry is known.
- Flyover: camera passes above the subject. Use for masterplan, roof, pool, courtyard, and development scale.
- Low Glide: drone-like camera moves low and smooth. Use for roads, gardens, pools, decks, and arrival shots.
- Dronie: camera rises and pulls away. Use as an ending reveal for houses, towers, resorts, and subdivisions.
- Helix: camera rises while orbiting. Use only with complete known geometry.
- Dive: camera descends rapidly. Use sparingly for energetic social openers.
- Fly-Through: camera passes through spaces. Use only if all connected spaces are shown or safe from supplied references.
- Rise And Tilt: camera rises while tilting from foreground to building. Use for strong exterior reveals.
- Descend And Approach: camera drops from aerial into entrance or amenity. Use when the route is visible and spatially plausible.
- Canopy Reveal: drone rises from behind trees, roofline, gate, or landscape to reveal the building.

## 7. Commercial Premium Moves

- Parallax Glide: smooth lateral movement with foreground depth. Use often for luxury archviz.
- Waterline Glide: camera moves close to water. Use for pools, reflecting pools, spas, fountains, and waterfront projects.
- Glidecam: elegant stabilized movement. Use for lobbies, interiors, social areas, terraces, and show units.
- Floating Camera: soft drifting camera. Use for calm premium interiors and amenity spaces.
- High-End Sweep: broad smooth movement across a hero scene. Use for final facade, pool, lobby, or living room moments.
- Material Detail Glide: camera slides across textures. Use for stone, wood, glass, metal, water, fabric, and lighting.
- Horizon Alignment Push: push toward a view while keeping horizon stable. Use for balconies, rooftops, ocean, mountain, city views.
- Amenity Ribbon Move: camera follows a pool edge, deck line, walkway, or landscape curve. Use to connect multiple amenity features.

## 8. Speed Effects

- Speed Ramp: movement accelerates or decelerates. Use to make social edits energetic while preserving readable hero moments.
- Reverse Ramp: starts slow then accelerates, or starts fast then slows. Use to land on architecture.
- Time Warp: stylized time distortion. Use rarely; can hurt fidelity.
- Hyperlapse: fast movement over distance. Use for location, access roads, site scale, or urban arrival.
- Slow Motion: slowed action. Use for water, fire, curtains, sunlight, people in lifestyle scenes, and luxury mood.
- Freeze: image freezes. Use for typography, feature callouts, or dramatic beat, not for Seedance realism unless requested.
- Stutter: short rhythmic repeated frames. Use for energetic social ads, not calm luxury films.
- Ramp-To-Cut: accelerate into a cut, then land slow on the next architectural hero shot.

## 9. Transitions

- Whip Pan: fast pan transition. Use for social edits, matching direction between spaces.
- Match Cut: cut between similar shapes, lines, materials, colors, or compositions.
- Jump Cut: abrupt cut. Use for energetic montage, not premium calm unless intentional.
- Cut On Action: cut during movement. Use when camera or subject motion continues across shots.
- L-Cut: previous audio continues into next shot. Use for music, ambience, voiceover, or water sound continuity.
- J-Cut: next audio begins before the image changes. Use for elegant anticipation.
- Light Sweep: light crosses frame as transition. Use for facade lighting, lobby lighting, sunlight, or glossy surfaces.
- Object Wipe: object passes across camera and hides the cut. Use columns, plants, walls, doors, furniture, curtains, or mullions.
- Architectural Line Match Cut: match vertical mullions to columns, pool edge to countertop, stair line to facade line.
- Texture Match Cut: match stone to stone, wood to wood, water to glass, concrete to facade panel.
- Doorway Portal Cut: transition through a doorway, gate, elevator, corridor, or lobby threshold.
- Window Or Mullion Wipe: use a window frame, glass rail, brise, or mullion as a wipe.
- Water Ripple Cut: use water movement to transition between pool, spa, waterfront, or reflection shots.
- Shadow Cut: pass through darkness or shade to hide a cut. Useful for night, lobby, cinema rooms, and moody interiors.
- Hard Cut With Sound Hit: clean cut on a low boom, click, whoosh, or music beat.
- Slow Dissolve: use only for calm luxury, sunset, memory, or soft sales-film pacing.

## 10. Sound Design Hooks

Default: no generated music unless the user explicitly asks for music. Use natural ambience and action-related sound effects. Add negative audio instructions: no music, no soundtrack, no song, no beat, no melody, no vocals.

- Soft whoosh: push-ins, pull-outs, glides, lateral tracks, object wipes.
- Low sub hit: hero facade reveal, logo/title moment, entrance reveal, final view.
- Airy riser: crane up, drone reveal, light sweep, transition into hero shot.
- Water shimmer: pool, spa, reflecting pool, fountain, waterline glide.
- Real water sound: pool ripples, cascade, spa, fountain, ocean nearby, waterline glide.
- Nature ambience: birds in the distance, palm leaves, coastal wind, garden ambience, horta/patio atmosphere.
- Glass tick or shimmer: facade glass, lobby, elevator, balcony rail, window reveal.
- Material foley: stone, wood, metal, fabric, door, curtain, gravel, footsteps, water.
- Clean snap: snap zoom, match cut, cut-on-action, feature callout.
- Wind bed: aerial, rooftop, balcony, exterior masterplan.
- Interior room tone: lobby, living room, suite, spa, gym, social area.
- Music beat cut: use only if the user explicitly requests music or music-synced editing.

## 11. Seedance Safety Tiers

Safe from one still/render:

- Static shot, lock-off, push in, pull out, zoom in, zoom out, slow zoom, small pan, small tilt, material detail glide, foreground reveal from visible objects.

Usually safe with strong references:

- Lateral truck, diagonal dolly, parallax reveal, threshold reveal, slider move, crane up, crane down, top-down, low glide, path trace, arc shot.

Needs complete spatial coverage:

- Orbit, bird orbit, helix, turnaround, fly-through, entry reveal into a new space, corner reveal into an unseen space, long walkthrough.

High risk for fidelity:

- Hyper orbit, crash zoom, dolly zoom, time warp, aggressive roll, dive, reverse angle, full 360, camera passing through walls, invented drone path around unseen sides.

## 12. Quick Style Recipes

Luxury Slow Reveal:

- Camera: slow push in, lateral truck, parallax glide, crane up, static hero.
- Transitions: match cut, light sweep, object wipe, slow dissolve.
- Sound: soft whoosh, low sub hit, airy riser, subtle ambience.

Fast Premium Reels:

- Camera: snap zoom, speed ramp, whip pan, push in, low glide, detail-to-wide reveal.
- Transitions: cut on action, match cut, object wipe, hard cut with sound hit.
- Sound: clean snaps, whooshes, beat cuts, short sub hits.

Drone Development Film:

- Camera: low glide, crane up, flyover, top-down, dronie, descend and approach.
- Transitions: match cut by roads, landscape lines, water, roofs, or horizon.
- Sound: wind bed, airy riser, low boom on site reveal.

Interior Material Film:

- Camera: macro detail glide, rack focus, tilt up, slider move, walkthrough glide, threshold reveal.
- Transitions: texture match cut, object wipe, doorway portal cut.
- Sound: soft foley, room tone, gentle whoosh, glass/stone shimmer.

Pool And Amenity Film:

- Camera: waterline glide, parallax reveal, slow push in, crane up, amenity ribbon move.
- Transitions: water ripple cut, light sweep, match cut on pool edge.
- Sound: water shimmer, soft splash, airy riser, low hit on hero frame.
