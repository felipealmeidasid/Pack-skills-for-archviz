# Narrative Patterns

Use this reference to choose a story arc for real estate videos and storyboard packages.

## Core Rule

Choose the pattern that best matches the available assets. If the user provides real project images, never invent spaces or angles just to complete a pattern. Adapt the pattern to visible references.

## High-End Development

1. Location / horizon / arrival.
2. Architecture hero.
3. Entrance or lobby.
4. Amenities and lifestyle.
5. Private interiors.
6. View / emotional closing.

Best for launches, premium buildings, mixed-use projects, branded residences, and high-ticket campaigns.

## Apartment Or Tower

1. Skyline or facade establishing shot.
2. Arrival / porte-cochere / lobby.
3. Social area or rooftop.
4. Living / kitchen / balcony.
5. Suite or intimate interior.
6. Final view or facade at golden hour.

Use strong continuity anchors: facade rhythm, lobby material, elevator/circulation logic, balcony/view, skyline.

## House

1. Approach through street, garden, gate, or driveway.
2. Exterior volume and material reveal.
3. Living integration.
4. Kitchen / dining / social space.
5. Outdoor leisure / pool / garden.
6. Sunset, night hero, or quiet final frame.

Use tactile transitions: gate to garden, stone to wood, living to terrace, pool to evening light.

## Subdivision Or Loteamento

1. Aerial/site establishing view.
2. Access and roads.
3. Lots, green areas, infrastructure, and water/landscape assets.
4. Amenity or community use.
5. Lifestyle/value proposition.
6. Masterplan or horizon closing.

Use site plans as spatial logic references. Do not turn plans into cinematic shots unless the user asks for a masterplan or plan-reveal moment.

## Interior Presentation

1. Entrance or first reveal.
2. Main living area.
3. Material/detail insert.
4. Kitchen/dining or social integration.
5. Bedroom/suite or private area.
6. Window view or final emotional composition.

Use detail beats to prove quality: stone, millwork, lighting, ceiling, fabric, metalwork, view, or tactile human gesture.

## Resort / Hospitality / Wellness

1. Landscape, water, or arrival ritual.
2. Architecture integrated with nature.
3. Lobby, reception, or service gesture.
4. Pool, spa, restaurant, or wellness amenity.
5. Suite, terrace, or private retreat.
6. Blue hour, candlelight, horizon, or calm closing shot.

Use natural ambient sound and slow-premium camera language unless the platform demands faster retention.

## Social Reel Structure

For a `9:16` Reel, prioritize retention:

1. First 0-3 seconds: strongest visual hook.
2. 3-10 seconds: architecture reveal and spatial promise.
3. 10-20 seconds: amenities, interior, or material proof.
4. 20-27 seconds: lifestyle/use proof.
5. 27-30 seconds: final hero shot or emotional close.

For 30 seconds, prefer 8-14 shots split across multiple storyboard boards.

## Presentation Film Structure

For a `16:9` sales film or client presentation:

1. Establish context.
2. Reveal architecture.
3. Explain arrival and circulation.
4. Show the strongest spaces.
5. Prove material/lifestyle value.
6. Close with identity, view, or facade.

Use slower pacing than social, but keep every shot purposeful.
